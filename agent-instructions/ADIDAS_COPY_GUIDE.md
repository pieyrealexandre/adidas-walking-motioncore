# adidas Copy Guidance – Main Guide

## Purpose
This is the master reference for generating adidas copy across all touchpoints. These guidelines come directly from adidas global brand standards and must be followed for all AI-generated content.

## Core Philosophy

**Try to find the emotion that connects.**

adidas exists in the space between sport and culture. That's where our stories live.

The writing should feel **human, grounded, and low key**. We never try too hard. We use fresh eyes — noticing the small things, the moments between people, what really matters about sport and the everyday.

---

## Universal Style & Language Rules

### Tone
- Keep it human, calm, and confident
- Low key, not overhyped
- Emotional, but not dramatic or over-the-top
- No rhymes, no big "manifesto" sentences. You can play with rhythm and melodic copy, but keep it natural
- Leave something to the imagination. Don't tell the whole story — leave room for the audience

### Brand Terms
- **adidas** is always written in lowercase, never "Adidas"
- **CONFIRMED** is always written in uppercase, never "Confirmed"

### Word Choices

**Minimize these overused words:**
- iconic, legacy, heritage

**Product terminology:**
- Shoes → use **silhouette** or **footwear**
- Clothing → use **apparel** or the specific item (hoodie, tee, shorts, etc.)

**Never use:**
- Slang or "online" language
- Cringe terms or forced sport slang (trying to sound "young" will have the opposite effect)

### Focus on the Consumer
Focus on the consumer's journey: their life, their everyday, how products fit into their routine.

Example phrases: "made for your life," "built for your everyday," "fits into your routine."

It's about what we bring to them, not about us talking about ourselves only.

Anchor copy in what they feel, where they wear it, how it moves with them.

---

## Storytelling & Structure

- **Leave something to the imagination**: don't explain everything
- Play with melodic, flowing copy, but avoid rhymes and overly dramatic sentences
- You can deviate slightly from existing copy when it feels right, but **never change technical product details**
  - This is especially important for sportswear — technical accuracy is crucial and a key legal risk area

---

## Claims & Risk

⚠️ **Critical Rules:**

- Never make up strong claims about a silhouette or product
- Don't invent performance benefits
- Always draw from existing product guidance or confirmed information
- If in doubt, focus on feel, comfort, versatility, or everyday use — **not performance promises**

---

## Big Drops & Collections

When writing for a big drop:
- Refer to the collection as a whole
- If there's a **hero product** (e.g., a specific silhouette), give it more stage time, especially on .com
- Make sure the hero is named clearly and appears in the headline or early in the copy where relevant

## Quick Reference: Copy Principles

### Attract and Engage Framework
- **Attract** = clear, simple hook (often the product or collection name)
- **Engage** = one short line that adds feeling or function

### Format Guidelines by Touchpoint

| Touchpoint | Headline | Body |
|------------|----------|------|
| Homepage Banner | 1–3 words | 1 short sentence |
| PLP Banner | Campaign name | 2–3 sentences with product experience |
| PDP Banner | Product/campaign name | Emotional narrative, 2–3 sentences |
| Push Notification | Key info, ultra-short | Single short line (optional) |
| Email Subject Line | Attention-grabbing | Pre-header elaborates |

---

## Content Generation Workflow

When generating copy, follow this sequence:

1. **Identify the touchpoint** → Use specialized guidelines
2. **Understand the consumer context** → What do they need at this moment?
3. **Check technical details** → Never invent product claims
4. **Apply tone rules** → Human, calm, confident, low key
5. **Verify brand terms** → adidas (lowercase), CONFIRMED (uppercase)
6. **Edit ruthlessly** → Less is more
7. **Read aloud** → Does it sound natural?
